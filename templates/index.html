Evidence overview

How much precision survives realistic attack prevalence?

This workbench reports one bounded experiment on CIC-IDS-2017: a Random Forest trained on Monday and Tuesday, thresholded on Wednesday, and scored once on Thursday and Friday. It shows the result, the failure modes, and the provenance behind every number, and it frames the open question as precision under realistic attack prevalence rather than benchmark accuracy.

Development evidence, not a blind confirmatory test The weekday results were inspected during development. They are useful for debugging and comparison, but they are not a preregistered confirmatory evaluation. The project does not claim deployment readiness, novel-attack detection, or live-capture efficacy.

Six questions this workbench answers

Each answer links to a view with the full evidence

1. What data, and which split?

CIC-IDS-2017, an academic dataset captured in a controlled laboratory VLAN. Training uses Monday and Tuesday ({{ "{:,}".format(research.splits.train.flows) }} flows). Wednesday is validation ({{ "{:,}".format(research.splits.validation.flows) }}). Thursday and Friday are the held-out test partition ({{ "{:,}".format(research.splits.test.flows) }}). The split is by day, not random, to avoid temporal leakage.

2. Which attack families were in training?

Only three labels: BENIGN, FTP-Patator, and SSH-Patator. Seven test families never appear in the training partition: Bot, DDoS, Infiltration, PortScan, and three Web Attack variants. Multiclass scores therefore collapse, and the binary attack score is the useful output.

3. What does the selected alert budget mean?

A budget is a review capacity, not a probability threshold. The model ranks flows by attack score and an analyst sees the top K. Fixed budgets cap alerts per interval; rate budgets set alerts per 10,000 flows. The alert budget view explains every predeclared budget and its measured precision and recall.

4. Where does the model fail?

It misses most Botnet flows ({{ pct(research.families[0].recall) }} at the frozen threshold), and its recall is uneven across families. PortScan is {{ pct(85286/158930) }}, DDoS is {{ pct(81380/128027) }}, and Infiltration is {{ pct(20/36) }} on only 36 flows. Multiclass macro-F1 is {{ "%.4f"|format(research.model.macro_f1) }}.

5. Is this real, sample, or prediction?

The dataset records are real labelled flows from a laboratory capture. The built-in samples are 1,500-row slices of that dataset. The flow table shows model predictions, not ground truth. The feedback experiment uses simulated labels, not human analysts. Live packet capture remains unvalidated.

6. Which model and features produced this?

Random Forest, seed 42, 80 CICFlowMeter features, scikit-learn {{ research.model.sklearn }} on Python {{ research.model.python }}. The model hash is recorded in the manifest. The Scapy upload parser now produces all 80 features. The PyFlowMeter live path maps all 80 names but dropped 2 of 6 fixture flows (ICMP and IPv6).

Budget is capacity, not a probability cut-off A fixed budget caps the number of alerts in an interval. A rate budget sets alerts per 10,000 flows. Neither is the same as the frozen probability threshold used in the baseline table.

Limitations and reproduction

What would have to be true before any deployment claim

The experiment is useful because its negative results are specific. This view lists the threats to validity, the claims the evidence does not support, and the commands needed to reproduce the artifacts.

Threats to validity

  • Development evidence, not confirmation. Weekday results were inspected during development. A confirmatory claim needs a preregistered protocol and an untouched final holdout.
  • Dataset realism. CIC-IDS-2017 was generated by scripted tools in a controlled VLAN. It does not represent encrypted enterprise traffic, diverse protocols, or realistic background noise.
  • Unseen families are not zero-day guarantees. The test families are absent from training, but they are known to the dataset authors and may share feature signatures with training attacks.
  • Threshold shift. A threshold frozen on Wednesday does not preserve a 1% false-positive rate on Thursday and Friday. The seed 42 RF test FPR is {{ pct(research.model.test.fpr) }}.
  • Small supports. Infiltration has 36 test flows and SQL Injection has 21. Their per-family recall estimates are unstable.
  • Serving output is uncalibrated. The serving Random Forest is explicitly registered as uncalibrated, so the console shows no confidence percentage. A calibration artifact exists, but its held-out ECE is about 0.208 and fails the 0.05 gate; calibration does not fix the ranking or alert budget.
  • Simulated feedback only. The feedback study assumes perfect labels and no delay. It is not a human factors study.
  • Live parity failure. {{ research.parity.missing_features }} features are missing and {{ research.parity.dummy_features }} are constants. Live capture cannot be claimed to reproduce the offline feature distribution.

Claims this evidence does not support

  • Deployment readiness, production intrusion detection, or real-time blocking.
  • Effective zero-day or novel-attack detection.
  • Improved detection from analyst feedback in a real SOC.
  • Validated accuracy on live network interfaces.
  • A calibrated probability that a given flow is an attack.
  • Superiority over commercial or production IDS products.
