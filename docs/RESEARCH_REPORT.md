## 1. Project Scope & Research Questions

This project investigates whether machine learning models trained on a publicly available network intrusion dataset can detect previously unseen attack classes when evaluated under realistic temporal constraints. The evaluation is designed to avoid common pitfalls of intrusion detection research (see §9).

**Core Research Questions:**

| # | Question | Phase |
|---|---|---|
| Q1 | Can a classifier detect attack families it was never trained on? | §3, §4 |
| Q2 | What happens to recall and false-alarm rate under realistic alert budgets? | §5 |
| Q3 | Does a small sample of analyst-labelled review traffic improve a model on subsequent unseen traffic? | §6 |
| Q4 | Can unsupervised drift monitoring warn of model degradation before human review? | §7 |
| Q5 | Is there feature-level parity between live packet capture and offline model training? | §8 |

## 3. Evaluation Protocol

**Protocol document:** [`docs/EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md`](EVALUATION_PROTOCOL.md)

### 3.1 Temporal Day-Based Split

Network flows exhibit strong temporal autocorrelation. Random train/test splits allow future information to leak into training, inflating reported F1 scores above 99%. This project uses a **strict temporal partition**:

```
Monday–Tuesday           Wednesday            Thursday–Friday
(Training Set)           (Validation Set)     (Test Set — HELD OUT)
 975,789 flows            692,686 flows         1,162,065 flows

 FTP-Patator              Threshold freeze      Web Attacks
 SSH-Patator              at Val FPR ≤ 1%       Infiltration
 BENIGN baseline                                Botnet
                                                PortScan
                                                DDoS
```

### 3.2 Generalization Tracks

| Track | Attack Families | In Training? |
|---|---|---|
| **Track A — Seen** | FTP-Patator, SSH-Patator | Yes (Mon–Tue) |
| **Track B — Unseen** | Web Attacks, Infiltration, Botnet, PortScan, DDoS | **Never** |

### 3.3 Evaluation Integrity Rules

1. **No test leakage:** Test set timestamps (Thu–Fri) are never examined before final scoring.
2. **Frozen thresholds:** Operating thresholds are frozen on Wednesday validation data at the maximum FPR ≤ 1.0% and are not re-tuned.
3. **Multi-seed replication:** All supervised models are trained across seeds {42, 43, 44, 45, 46}. Mean and standard deviation are reported.
4. **Binary reformulation:** Attack/BENIGN binary labels are derived from the original multi-class labels. Macro-F1 is reported but noted as misleading given zero training coverage of test-only families.

### 4.2 Key Findings

**Finding 1 — Multiclass F1 Collapse:**  
Macro-F1 scores (RF: 0.0857, LR: 0.0853) are near-zero because Track B attack families (Web Attacks, Botnet, PortScan, DDoS) have zero training examples. Supervised classifiers cannot assign categorical labels to unseen classes. Binary PR-AUC (0.747 for RF) is the only operationally meaningful metric.

**Finding 2 — Threshold Brittleness:**  
Thresholds frozen at Wednesday validation FPR ≤ 1.0% show severe threshold slippage on test traffic:
- RF: observed test FPR rises to 4.11% (4× the validation target)
- LR: observed test FPR is 2.66% (2.7× the target)

This demonstrates that offline threshold selection does not guarantee false alarm bounds under distribution shift.

**Finding 3 — Unsupervised Baseline:**  
Isolation Forest achieves ROC-AUC 0.676 without any labels during training, confirming statistical anomaly separability in flow features. Its near-zero Recall@1%FPR (0.0046) reflects that the threshold calibration problem is worse for unsupervised scores.

### 4.3 Unseen-Family and Botnet Experiment (D6)

The D6 experiment tested seven predeclared arms on the frozen day-based split. Training contained only `FTP-Patator` and `SSH-Patator`; every final-test family, including Bot, was unseen. No arm reached the Bot gate at ≤1% FPR.

| Class weighting | 1.86% ± 1.72% | 0.286 ± 0.280 | 12.5% ± 11.6% | 0.27% ± 0.61% | 0.207 ± 0.148 |
| Family-aware augmentation | 8.65% ± 1.37% | 0.605 ± 0.304 | 29.4% ± 12.4% | 20.7% ± 16.6% | 0.179 ± 0.085 |
| Isolation Forest | 0.95% ± 0.46% | 0.004 ± 0.003 | 0.73% ± 0.37% | 0.00% | 0.288 ± 0.011 |
| PCA reconstruction | 2.27% | 0.115 | 5.97% | 2.19% | 0.207 |

## 5. Alert Burden Evaluation (Phase 4)

**Design document:** [`docs/ALERT_BURDEN_AND_FEEDBACK.md`](ALERT_BURDEN_AND_FEEDBACK.md)  
**Code:** `training/evaluate.py` → `evaluate_alert_budgets()`

### 5.1 Motivation

Traditional detection metrics assume unbounded analyst review capacity. Real SOC tier-1 analysts inspect approximately 20–50 alerts per shift. This phase evaluates models by ranking flows by attack score and presenting the Top-K alerts, mirroring real queue-based triage.

### 5.2 Predeclared Budgets (declared before examining test results)

| Specification | Type | Analyst Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Top-50 alerts | Fixed volume | Minimum solo shift capacity |
| Top-100 alerts | Fixed volume | Standard two-analyst shift |
| Top-250 alerts | Fixed volume | Small team queue |
| Top-500 alerts | Fixed volume | Team queue with 10 analysts |
| 10 alerts per 10,000 flows | Rate-based | Light monitoring density |
| 50 alerts per 10,000 flows | Rate-based | Standard enterprise density |
| 100 alerts per 10,000 flows | Rate-based | High-vigilance density |

### 5.3 Results (Random Forest Seed 42, Friday test partition)

| Budget | K | Precision@K | Attack Recall | FP/10k Benign | Botnet Recall | DDoS Recall | PortScan Recall |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Top-50 | 50 | 100.00% | 0.017% | 0.00 | 0.00% | 0.00% | 0.031% |
| Top-100 | 100 | 100.00% | 0.035% | 0.00 | 0.00% | 0.00% | 0.063% |
| Top-250 | 250 | 100.00% | 0.087% | 0.00 | 0.00% | 0.00% | 0.157% |
| Top-500 | 500 | 99.60% | 0.172% | 0.05 | 0.00% | 0.00% | 0.313% |
| 10 per 10,000 | 703 | 98.15% | 0.239% | 0.31 | 0.00% | 0.00% | 0.434% |
| 50 per 10,000 | 3,516 | 97.50% | 1.186% | 2.12 | 0.00% | 1.143% | 1.236% |
| 100 per 10,000 | 7,032 | 98.73% | 2.403% | 2.15 | 0.051% | 3.864% | 1.255% |

**Key Finding:** At any operationally realistic budget, the alert queue contains near-zero Botnet detections. Even at 100 alerts per 10,000 flows, Botnet recall is 0.051% (roughly one in two thousand Botnet flows) while DDoS recall reaches 3.86%. The model exhausts its review budget on high-confidence PortScan and DDoS flows, leaving Botnet effectively invisible. (Infiltration is not part of the Friday budget evaluation; its Thursday per-family recall is reported separately.) Precision stays at or above 97.5%, but recall remains in the low single digits: the queue is pure and misses the vast majority of attacks.

## 6. Simulated Analyst Feedback (Phase 5)

**Design document:** [`docs/ALERT_BURDEN_AND_FEEDBACK.md`](ALERT_BURDEN_AND_FEEDBACK.md)  
**Code:** `training/simulate_feedback.py`  
**Runner:** `scripts/windows/run_feedback_simulation.bat`

### 6.1 Research Question

Does a small analyst-labelled review budget on Thursday traffic (458,825 flows) help the model generalise better to Friday unseen attacks (703,240 flows)?

### 6.2 Experimental Design

**Time-ordered partition:**

```
Mon–Tue (Train) → Wed (Val threshold freeze) → Thu (Review period) → Fri (Untouched eval)
```

**Selection policies under budget B ∈ {50, 100, 250}:**

- **No-Feedback (B=0):** Baseline; model deployed as-is to Friday.
- **Random sampling:** Uniform random sample of B Thursday flows.
- **Top-Alert:** Highest B attack scores (mirrors SOC queue triage).
- **Uncertainty sampling:** B flows closest to decision boundary (active learning).

### 6.3 Results (5 Seeds, 50 Total Runs)

| Policy | Budget B | Friday PR-AUC | Recall at frozen threshold | Test FPR | Test precision | Botnet recall | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| No feedback | 0 | 0.8561 ± 0.0530 | 0.5805 ± 0.1489 | 0.0061 ± 0.0024 | 0.9850 ± 0.0057 | 0.94% ± 0.82% | Reference |
| Random sampling | 50 | 0.8083 ± 0.0312 | 0.4513 ± 0.0776 | 0.0072 ± 0.0026 | 0.9766 ± 0.0115 | 0.72% ± 0.89% | Worse |
| Top-alert triage | 50 | 0.8467 ± 0.1152 | 0.5889 ± 0.2777 | 0.0110 ± 0.0058 | 0.9668 ± 0.0298 | 1.22% ± 1.02% | Inconclusive |
| Uncertainty sampling | 50 | 0.8585 ± 0.0500 | 0.6008 ± 0.1324 | 0.0125 ± 0.0054 | 0.9699 ± 0.0141 | 0.78% ± 0.57% | Within noise |
| Random sampling | 100 | 0.8458 ± 0.0556 | 0.5559 ± 0.1600 | 0.0071 ± 0.0038 | 0.9830 ± 0.0055 | 1.01% ± 0.90% | Worse |
| Top-alert triage | 100 | 0.8148 ± 0.0814 | 0.4931 ± 0.2129 | 0.0118 ± 0.0053 | 0.9612 ± 0.0217 | 1.09% ± 1.06% | Worse |
| Uncertainty sampling | 100 | 0.8610 ± 0.0471 | 0.6071 ± 0.1312 | 0.0135 ± 0.0087 | 0.9681 ± 0.0212 | 0.90% ± 0.83% | Within noise |
| Random sampling | 250 | 0.7971 ± 0.0531 | 0.4376 ± 0.1368 | 0.0097 ± 0.0063 | 0.9675 ± 0.0228 | 1.10% ± 0.78% | Worse |
| Top-alert triage | 250 | 0.8276 ± 0.0696 | 0.5355 ± 0.1715 | 0.0194 ± 0.0131 | 0.9451 ± 0.0514 | 20.35% ± 10.62% | High-variance outlier |
| Uncertainty sampling | 250 | 0.8343 ± 0.0146 | 0.5294 ± 0.0451 | 0.0131 ± 0.0061 | 0.9664 ± 0.0143 | 0.93% ± 0.77% | Worse |

**Key Finding:** No policy beats the no-feedback reference by a margin larger than the seed-to-seed spread. The best mean is uncertainty sampling at B=100 (PR-AUC 0.8610 versus 0.8561), which is a 0.005 difference against a standard deviation of roughly 0.05. Uncertainty sampling at B=250 falls to 0.8343 and Botnet recall stays at 0.93%. The single top-alert B=250 cell reports 20.35% Botnet recall, but its PR-AUC is 0.8276 with a 10.62-point Botnet spread and a 0.0696 PR-AUC spread; it is an unstable outlier, not a reliable improvement. Botnet remains effectively undetected across the grid, confirming that passive sampling from a single intermediate day does not address the feature-space gap.

**Uncertainty note:** All values are mean ± standard deviation across seeds 42–46. No confidence intervals or significance tests were computed. The feedback experiment therefore supports a negative result — the simulated labels did not produce a clear, stable improvement — rather than a ranking of policies.

> [!WARNING]
> **Simulation vs. Human Studies:** This is an *offline retrospective simulation*. Labels provided to the model are assumed 100% accurate and instantaneous. Real analyst feedback involves label noise, ambiguous telemetry, delayed triage, and cognitive fatigue. These results cannot be claimed as evidence for real human-in-the-loop deployment effectiveness.

## 7. Drift Monitoring (Phase 6)

**Design document:** [`docs/DRIFT_MONITORING.md`](DRIFT_MONITORING.md)  
**Code:** `training/drift_monitor.py`  
**Runner:** `scripts/windows/run_drift_monitor.bat`

### 7.1 Methodology

Unsupervised drift detection using:
- **Score PSI (Population Stability Index):** Tracks shifts in model output score distributions across chronological windows.
- **2-sample Kolmogorov–Smirnov test:** Applied to 6 high-importance flow features across consecutive day-splits.

**Integrity rule:** No test labels are used to trigger drift warnings. Labels are only used retrospectively to audit warning quality.

### 7.2 Chronological Drift Audit

| Window | Flagged Features | Warning? | True Degradation? | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Tue KnownAttacks | 2 / 6 | Yes | No (Recall=1.000) | False Warning |
| **Wed DoS/Heartbleed** | **6 / 6** | **Yes** | **Yes (Recall=0.000)** | **True Warning** |
| Thu AM WebAttacks | 0 / 6 | No | Yes (Recall=0.000) | **Missed Degradation** |
| Thu PM Infiltration | 5 / 6 | Yes | No | False Warning |
| Fri AM Botnet | 1 / 6 | No | Yes | **Missed Degradation** |
| **Fri PM PortScan** | **6 / 6** | **Yes** | **Yes** | **True Warning** |
| **Fri PM DDoS** | **6 / 6** | **Yes** | **Yes** | **True Warning** |

**Summary:** 3 True Warnings, 2 False Warnings, 2 Missed Degradations, 0 Correctly Quiet.

**Key Finding:** Unsupervised drift monitoring is most reliable for volumetric attacks (DDoS, PortScan) that induce large statistical shifts across all monitored features. Stealth attacks (Web Attacks, Botnet) produce minimal feature distribution shifts, evading KS-test detection.

> [!NOTE]
> Drift warnings should trigger model audit and human review. They must not be used to automatically adjust firewall rules or block traffic.

The following features are absent from the Scapy upload parser:

Fwd/Bwd PSH Flags, URG Flags
Fwd/Bwd Header Length

| Root Cause | Impact |

> [!CAUTION]
> **Live capture inference is UNVALIDATED.** The 28.8% feature gap, missing TCP window statistics, and dummy active/idle values make live-capture predictions incomparable to offline model training. The Flask application labels live capture as a **research prototype** and must not claim validated intrusion detection accuracy on live traffic.

## 9. Explicit Limitations

### 9.1 Dataset Limitations

- CIC-IDS-2017 is a **laboratory-generated capture**, not traffic from a real organisational network. It is real labelled data, but the attacks were scripted in a controlled VLAN.
- Attack traffic is scripted; real attacks involve C2 randomisation, TLS obfuscation, and adaptive evasion.
- Class imbalance is severe: benign traffic represents roughly 80% of all flows; some attack families have fewer than 2,000 examples, and Infiltration has only 36 test flows.
- The dataset's labels are the dataset authors' labels, not independent ground truth. Label errors and ambiguous flows were not audited.

### 9.2 Model Limitations

- **No multi-class generalisation:** Supervised models cannot label attack families not present in training. Binary attack/benign scoring is the only meaningful output for the seven unseen test families.
- **Threshold instability:** A threshold frozen on Wednesday validation at maximum FPR 1% does not preserve that bound on Thursday and Friday. The five-seed RF mean test FPR is 4.11% ± 3.27%; seed 42 is 0.73%, so the outcome varies sharply by seed.
- **No calibrated confidence on the serving path:** The served Random Forest is registered as uncalibrated and the console shows no confidence percentage. The available isotonic artifact fails the 0.05 ECE gate. D5 evaluated isotonic calibration on the binary attack score for this RF configuration; it improves Brier score but held-out ECE remains high and varies by day and family, so calibration does not repair ranking or the alert budget. The multiclass Brier score is 0.4959 and the 10-bin expected calibration error is 0.2477 for seed 42. See [CALIBRATION_V2.md](CALIBRATION_V2.md).
- **Botnet near-invisibility:** Botnet recall is 1.12% at the frozen threshold (22 of 1,966 flows). Across the feedback grid it stays near 1% except for one high-variance top-alert B=250 cell (20.35% ± 10.62%), which is not a reliable improvement.
- **Serving artifact identity is fixed:** the registry-active serving artifact is the evaluated Random Forest seed 42 bundle, verified by SHA-256. It is still a three-class model trained on BENIGN, FTP-Patator, and SSH-Patator, applies the recorded attack-score threshold (`1 - P(BENIGN) >= 0.002515048908954043`), and retains the argmax label for audit. It is registered as uncalibrated. Artifact identity is a provenance fix, not a detection gain.

### 9.3 Evaluation Limitations

- **Development evidence, not confirmation:** The weekday results were inspected during development. They are not a preregistered blind test. A confirmatory claim needs a protocol fixed before the final holdout is scored.
- **Uncertainty reporting:** The tables report mean ± standard deviation across seeds 42–46. No confidence intervals, significance tests, or correction for multiple comparisons were applied. Differences smaller than the seed spread should be treated as inconclusive.
- **Phase 5 is a simulation, not a human study:** The feedback experiment assumes every selected Thursday flow is labelled instantly and correctly. Real analysts face ambiguous telemetry, label noise, delay, and fatigue.
- **Drift monitoring does not generalise to stealthy attacks:** The audit produced three true warnings, two false warnings, and two missed degradations. Web attacks and Botnet produced little feature-distribution shift.
- **Feature parity audit covers Python Scapy only:** Parity against Suricata, Zeek, or nProbe was not tested. The upload parser now produces all 80 training features, but the live path drops ICMP/IPv6 and the two extractors disagree numerically; live predictions are unvalidated.

### 9.4 Deployment Limitations

- This codebase is a **research prototype** and not a security product.
- It should not be deployed in production networks, used for automated blocking, or cited as evidence of commercial threat detection capability.

## 10. Cost-Sensitive Evaluation and Release Gate (D9)

**Status:** complete; release gate **FAIL**.
**Report:** [`COST_MODEL_AND_RELEASE_GATE.md`](COST_MODEL_AND_RELEASE_GATE.md)  
**Machine-readable result:** [`COST_SENSITIVE_RESULTS.json`](COST_SENSITIVE_RESULTS.json)  
**Script:** `scripts/cost_sensitive_eval.py`

A detector is not useful in the abstract. It is useful when the cost of the false positives it creates, plus the cost of the attacks it misses, is lower than doing nothing. D9 defines `C_FP` as one analyst review (normalised to 1), `C_FN` as one missed attack, and reports the sensitivity curve over `r = C_FN / C_FP`. No organization supplied real costs, so the results are a ratio curve, not a dollar ROI. Review time is an illustrative 5 minutes per alert.

**Frozen-threshold results (Thursday+Friday, 1,162,065 flows):**

| Model | FPR | Recall | Precision | Alert rate | Review h/10k | Break-even `r` | Cost @ r=10 per 10k |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| LR (legacy) | 2.66% | 3.52% | 0.307 | 2.88% | 24.0 | **3.26** | 24,459 |
| RF (served, 5 seeds) | 4.11% ± 3.27% | 58.30% ± 14.79% | 0.837 | 17.68% | 147.4 | **1.21** | 12,216 |
| Isolation Forest | 1.16% | 0.46% | 0.087 | 0.99% | 8.2 | unstable | 25,037 |
| No detector | — | 0% | — | 0% | 0 | — | 25,054 |

**Fixed alert-budget results (100 alerts per 10,000 flows):**

| Model | Alerts | Precision | Recall | Break-even `r` | Cost @ r=100 per 10k |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| LR (legacy) | 11,621 | 20.3% | 0.81% | **4.93** | 248,606 |
| RF (served) | 11,621 | 90.3% | 3.61% | **1.11** | 241,604 |
| Isolation Forest | 11,621 | 9.3% | 0.37% | 151.5 | 249,711 |
| No detector | 0 | — | 0% | — | 250,536 |

The served Random Forest is a real, if narrow, cost-competitive ranker under a fixed queue: at 100 alerts/10k it reaches 90.3% precision and breaks even at `r≈1.11`, but it recovers only 3.6% of attacks.
